IoT and embedded systems · Instrumentation

Low-cost field instruments

  • Period2014–2020
  • StatusPublished
  • RoleCo-author and instrument developer
  • OrganizationMa Chung Research Center for Photosynthetic Pigments
  • ToolsWeb camera, Raspberry Pi, Arduino, Android

Laboratory instruments are often too expensive for field stations and small research groups. This line of work built simple alternatives from off-the-shelf parts.

A modified web camera (2015) imaged soybean plants from above. Its NDVI-like index reached a coefficient of determination of 0.70 against a SPAD meter, with a leaf-area error of 18.41 percent. The same sensor was used to screen drought-tolerant soybean, where a neural network reached about 80 percent accuracy.

A low-cost chlorophyll meter (2016) combines red and near-infrared LEDs with a photodiode and sends readings to an Android app. On 20 cassava leaves it reached a coefficient of determination of 0.9681 and an RMSE of 0.014 against a SPAD-502.

PlantAnalyzer (2019) is a Raspberry Pi multispectral imager that maps NDVI across a whole leaf. A teaching photometer (2020), built from a 633 nm LED, a TSL250 photodiode, and an Arduino Uno, matched a laboratory spectrophotometer with a coefficient of determination of 0.98.
