Three published devices are the record for this line of work.

A modified web camera, reported in 2015, imaged soybean plants from above. Its NDVI-like index had a coefficient of determination of 0.70 against a SPAD meter, and leaf-area error was 18.41 percent. The same sensor family was used to screen drought-tolerant soybean. A neural network on those measurements reached about 80 percent accuracy in the 2015 Procedia Chemistry paper.

The low-cost chlorophyll meter, reported in 2016, combines red and near-infrared LEDs with a photodiode and sends the reading to an Android app. On 20 cassava leaves the paper reports a coefficient of determination of 0.9681 and an RMSE of 0.014 against a SPAD-502.

A 2019 paper describes PlantAnalyzer, a Raspberry Pi multispectral imager that maps NDVI across a whole leaf. A 2020 teaching photometer, built with a 633 nm LED, a TSL250 photodiode, and an Arduino Uno, matched a laboratory spectrophotometer with a coefficient of determination of 0.98 on the samples in that paper.

These figures are the ones printed in the papers. They are not a new test.
